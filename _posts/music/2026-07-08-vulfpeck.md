This one is probably a bit overdue to be honest.

I was listening to a youtube earth wind and fire playlist, eventually I was listening so long that they started playing some stuff from other artists, most of which I skipped, to go back to EW&F. Then the algorithm served up [Tee time](https://youtu.be/iFG18LOlj-o?si=4X4dp1iTZdAnArOW). I was ready to skip again, but I was surprised at how much I enjoyed it. Very simple and pared back, but catchy. Also very lo-fi in sound and production. So much that I made the mistake of thinking they were some casual hobbyists.

Nertheless I took a quick look on Spotify (which was the style at the time), and listened to the Live at Madison Square Garden album while I took a walk down the river by bushy park. It was a good walk. I've listened to pretty much all of their stuff, which is quite a lot now. There's also Vulfmon, which is a solo side-quest project of the band's leader, Jack Stratton.

What I like about Vulfpeck, other than their music, is how much they've taught me about music, particularly about past musicians, and modern music making. A lot of the fun facts or obscure names I've learnt over the last few years have usually stemmed from Vulfpeck in one way or another.

For example, Cory Wong, who has an 'On the One' YouTube series where he breaks down some of his tracks, and how he created them. It's just a really good resource into learning what goes into making music. 

[It gets funkier IV](https://youtu.be/Nq5LMGtBmis?si=78GjH2aa0H6kHtu9) was my first introduction to Louis Cole. 

The Fearless Flyers were another side project, where I found Nate Smith.

Also learning about mixing, there's some stuff I've already done on different mixes of the same song, so I won't repeat, but I learned it from a Vulfpeck video. What I like is they take the music VERY seriously, but are pretty silly about everything else.

[Funklet](https://youtu.be/7g94qfntzF4?si=WYeFq1pGL4jJlLoO)

[Sky mall](https://youtu.be/KaBBWWj5h_c?si=DbKj_MPg26sleUtp) wait for the keyboard stand leg to fall over perfectly on beat.

[Wait for the moment](https://youtu.be/r4G0nbpLySI?si=ATNwnh0QG84II_h8). The moment in question is the drum fill where Theo basically does nothing. Around 2:10. Vulfpeck are often described as 'living room funk', this is a pretty good example of why.

I could go on forever, but I'll leave you with the video of their first [MSG concert](https://youtu.be/rv4wf7bzfFE?si=U33E81okq6pyenO-), which was done with one camera in one continuous shot. I've watched this so many times. There's so many great little bits, but the music itself is what I come back to. They're all very talented musicians, despite the sometimes unserious appearance. A very good gateway into other cool music that's being made out there, it's not all AI nonsense.
