This was a cool day, one that I'd been looking forward to for a while.

A friend from Japanese school asked if I wanted to come to pokepark Kanto, which is about an hour and a bit away from Tokyo. He bought three tickets so my friend from home could come too.

We met at the station after I yelled at my friend for once again being late. He wasn't really that late but I just felt he needed yelling at.

Next we met at Kudanshita and took the train, then a acable car, to the pokepark.

After a bit of queueing and waiting around for it to open, we made it inside. The pokepark is actually inside another theme park, which is where we waited for the pokepark to open. I had a gyoza hot dog for breakfast. It was pretty good, like a long steamed bun with a giant gyoza inside. 

We grabbed some drinks and headed inside. You've got to book certain slots for certain areas too, which is a bit of a niusance but oh well. First was the shop, where I bought a few overpriced bits and bobs. I must say, credit where it's due, pokemon merchandise is an excellent money spinner.

Next we went inside the main park and walked around. I'll let the pictures do the talking, when I get round to uploading them. Maybe tomorrow or tonight. It's already gone midnight so I guess it'll have to be today or tonight.

There's a main park which has a pokemon centre, a mart, loads of pokemon all over the place, some stalls, places to sit, a little battle arena, a fountain and loads of statues and photo opportunities. The legendary kanto birds statue was the best, but hard to take good photos of. You sort of need to see it from all angles.

We went to a show too, which was probably more for kids but still pretty entertaining. I think there was a plot there but mainly was just some people dancing around, some of whom were dressed as giant pikachu.

Then there's the forest, I forget the exact name. It's supposed to resemble route 1 of the kanto games, which is really cool. They've got loads of pokemon along the route, so it's basically an exercise in walking around taking photos of a manufactured wilderness. Usually it's the sort of thing I'm pretty cynical about, but hey it's pokemon so I like it.

We chilled out for a bit waiting for a parade to start, luckily our seats were in a pretty good spot already so we didn't have to move for the parade.

After the parade we were ready to head home. We got really lucky with the weather, clear skies all day after having weeks of rain and cloud. It did make us thirsty though.

We headed back after a long but enjoyable day, our wallets lighter but our inner child satisfied. Dinner was in Shinjuku, at Saizeriya. It's Japanese Italian food and it's kinda hilarious. The prices are pretty cheap, so you usually get 2 or 3 dishes. I had a pea and egg salad, hamburg steak, and garlic bread. Also drink bar. Drink bar is mandatory on a day like this.

This was probably one of the most memorable days in Japan.
