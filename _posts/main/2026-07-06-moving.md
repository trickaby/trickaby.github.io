So it's been a short while since my last post, again I'm doing a fairly bad job of keeping up with a regular schedule.  But we're here now which is the important thing.

Last week I moved house! I was living in a flat by myself in Ōkubo, which is between Shinjuku and Takadanobaba. It was nice because I could walk to both places from my flat, which is useful for socialising in Shinjuku, and going to school in Takadanobaba. Rent was pretty steep though and I was never going to stay there beyond 3 months. 

Now I'm living in Nihombashi, which is near to Tokyo station, Ginza, Akihabara. That kind of area. I now have to take the train to Takadanobaba, and it takes about 40 minutes instead of my half hour walk from Ōkubo. Still not a bad journey though. Just one train the whole way.

The move itself I found a little bit stressful. Not because the process itself was particularly complicated or taxing, I just stress out easily. The day before my move, I had to go to Shinjuku city hall to fill out a moving out notification form. It took maybe an hour and a half or so, most of which was spent just sitting around waiting. Admin is actually something I don't think Japan does very well. Everything is still very paper based it seems. A lot of these processes I'd expect to just be an online form these days.

On the move day itself, I went to the office of the company I'm renting my room from. They gave me a few bits of information, got me to sign my contract, and gave me my key. I should have gone with my luggage because it was on the way to my new place, but unfortunately I was being thick so left everything at home.

Moving all my stuff took 2 trips. I've only got one suitcase, so first trip was moving all my belongings, second trip was moving all of my flat's belongings. Dish soap and soy sauce, that kind of thing. Wasn't too bad, said hi to a few housemates as I was moving in. 

That night was England vs DR Congo, a 1am kickoff. Happily, 2 of my housemates were keen to watch the game with me, which gave us a good amount of time to get to know one another. I was a bit too busy watching the game to make much chat though.

Last week was also my break from scool, during which I'd planned to get out and about a bit more. It didn't really happen, I was feeling a bit stressed out about the upcoming move, and maybe a bit worn out from the previous term of school, saying bye to friends, worrying about the upcoming term. If I have too many things on my mind, it isn't great. Having made the move, I'm much more relaxed now.

As a result writing this is a lot easier. I don't have much else to talk about. Most of my days on holiday were filled watching the world cup, studying, and eating through what remained in my fridge. I stopped exercising for a while which isn't good. In my defence the weather was being rubbish pretty much every day. I didn't want to go out in the rain to do pullups.

Actually in retrospect, maybe I'm just feeling better because the weather is nicer. It hasn't rained much since I've moved, so I've been able to go out a bit more. The rain really does affect my day; being out in the rain is rubbish.

I did have a couple of days out. One was a school organised tram trip, where we accompanied a group of families with their young children. I got to do a bit of Japanese practice which was nice, but the weather was pretty bad. Also went out for karaoke and sushi another day, which is always good fun. I think I'm yet to find my signature karaoke song.

So, Nihombashi. I'm living in a sharehouse, which is a mix of guys and girls, Japanese and foreigners. I think it's a nice way of doing things. You get to meet some Japanese people, practice the language, they get to practice English, and the gender mix encourages people to be a bit more well behaved and tidy, I reckon. I got a bit lucky, my room was being vacated the day before my flat's contract ended, so I could move in straight away. When I enquired about somewhere to live, this was their first suggestion so I just jumped at it, not wanting the listing to get taken.

The location is really nice. Seems more like a business district, compared to my old place which was more of a crowded city centre type place. The streets were too narrow for the amount of people, and I never really got the chance to walk around the streets slowly and take everything in. Nihombashi seems better for that, with a few interesting shops and cafes and the like. It just seems a bit less dirty and crowded too. Not that I didn't like Ōkubo, but maybe it wasn't quite right for me. There will be things I miss from there I'm sure. But so far I'm enjoying my new digs. It's also really nice to be able to walk to some of the more swanky parts of Tokyo. You can just go for a walk without feeling the pressure of interacting with any of it. I've also found a few nice park spots. Ōkubo was really good for that to be fair. 

There doesn't seem to be much in the way of outdoor exercise equipment here, so I'll probably sign up to a gym. I'm getting a bit small to be honest so maybe it's just as well.

There was also a party for someone's birthday, which was mixed in with a leaving party for a few people who will be moving out in the coming weeks. Someone else also moved in a few days after me, so there seems to have been a fair amount of turnover recently. It was a nice evening, just sat around chatting and eating yakisoba. Then some of us went for a walk around town. It was cool being out at night, something I always enjoy but rarely do.

I think I still need to figure out a sleep schedule, because it would be nice to chat to people a bit more and get to know them, also practice my Japanese. But it seems like people are a bit more active later in the day than my usual time, around 10 or 11. So it might mean me pushing back my bedtime a little.

I have more to talk about but that's probably enough for now
