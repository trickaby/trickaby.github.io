This weekend I'd lined up a trip to Kochi with a sharehouse friend and some former tenants that I didn't know. I was pretty tired from the week's adventures though, and my heart wasn't really in it. Anyway, I left on time to go to the airport, went to the wrong station, misread my google maps. Got on a train and ended up near Yokohama, missing my flight. I message my friends to tell them, and they said I'd be able to get it cancelled or changed if I called them up. I was sceptical and honestly just wanted to go home and go back to bed. Anyway, I got out of a station to make the call, and surprisingly they just put me on the next flight at no extra cost. Even though it was all my fault. It was also after my flight's departure time, so I was very pleasantly surprised.

Anyway, I finally got to the airport onto my new flight. Unfortunately I'd missed the best of the weather in Kochi, where my friends headed to a river which looked really nice. If I'd have made my original flight they would have picked me up from the airport and we'd have gone together. Oh well eh.

When I arrived, I took a bus into town. I had time to kill, so just wandered around, then headed to Kochi castle, but it was about to close. I just walked around the grounds and found a nice bench where I sat and took in the amazing views. Kochi is by the sea but also surrounded by mountains, one of the nicest places you could find yourself reluctantly ending up in.

I'd also done nothing to plan for this trip, I had no time. I had nothing to look forward to and no real expectations. So everything was just a bonus.

I could hear some music coming up from below the castle grounds, so I went to investigate. Apparently there was a concert going on, live under kochi castle. I stayed for a bit and really enjoyed it. Very lively and colourful with a great variety of performances.

I met with my friends in town for dinner, at an izakaya. I had fish organs. It wasn't delicious. I had chicken wings. It was delicious. We had some other stuff too that fit somewhere in between the two in terms of deliciousness. We then headed back to the concert, then back back to the food court for some more food and drink. They also have this charred tuna thing which is a local favourite. It came on a model boat too which was fun. Also got some really goopy (compliment) takoyaki. 

We headed back to our friends house, who had kindly agreed to let us stay at theirs'.

The next day was very wet. We went to a Sunday market, which apparently has produce from all over Kochi prefecture. Sorry I should explain we were in Kochi city which is in Kochi prefecture, in Shikoku.

The market was good but it was really rainy and my overwhelming take away was wet feet.

Next was Kochi castle, this time I went inside the castle. The view from the top was amazing, and there was a nice model of what the castle grounds would have looked like back in the day. One of the people we were staying with is English, and he was really helpful with explaining stuff to me about the history of Kochi and the castle.

Lunch was ramen, I had curry ramen. It was nice, I had been meaning to try curry ramen too. The charshu was particularly good.

After lunch we went to a cave, which was a really interesting experience. Essentially it was a one way path in, around, and out. I was taken aback by how it just completely felt like I was in a video game. 

A few things to explain. A lot of video games are linear story based, meaning you just follow the story as it goes along, like a movie or a book or something. You don't get much choice in where you go, you just have to follow the story the game intended. You have other games like open world RPGs, where it's more exploratory and you can just go wherever. You also have fifa which is a football sim.

Anyway, in a lot of linear story games, as well as some RPGs in parts, you often find yourself in caves. It makes sense, it's a closed environment, where you can easily set a linear path that gives the player no choice in where they go. It's also a natural environment so you don't need to do any work in making it "believable". For example if your game takes place in a city, you have to build loads of road blocks and dead ends to stop the player getting off course.

It's also dark and spooky which can be useful for certain games.

You've also got loading screens, where a game has to load the new map. What games have started doing though, is having interactive loading screens where the player doesn't realise they're in one. A common trope is having the character squeeze through a narrow passage, slowing down as they struggle to get through. While they shuffle along, the next part of the cave loads in. Then they make it through, and the newly loaded map is seemlessly ready, without any cut in the apparent gameplay. I think it's pretty clever, but then once you're aware of it, it makes the game a bit less magical.

Anyway, this cave had a few of those shuffley sections, and me and my new English friend both had the same thought, that they're just buying time to load the next section of cave. Also some of the bits of the cave had funny names. My favourite was "the rock where the emporer scratches his belly button". What does that even mean.

After the cave I was driven to the airport to go back home. It was a very short stay in Kochi, but I'm still glad I went. I'm always a bit nervous and shy around new people but they were very friendly so I enjoyed myself. They were also very generous with their time, especially with a complete stranger in me.

From the airport I flew home, did my daily study, and started writing these blogs. Now we're all up to date!

I have no idea what I'm doing the rest of the week. Nothing planned. Hopefully I can rest a bit then do cool stuff
