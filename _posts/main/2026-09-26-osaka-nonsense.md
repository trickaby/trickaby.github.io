Ok so first things first, Namba station is a maze and whoever is in charge of signposts needs to finish the job.

I eventually got to Kansai after getting lost in Namba, where I was just nicely early so I didn't have to rush or panic, but I didn't have to wait too long. Picked up my friend from home and we headed into town. The nearest station to our accoms was Namba, so we got lost for a bit then headed over to drop luggage, then get some late night ramen. It was really good ramen and of course I got some gyoza on the side too. So far so good.

By the way read the previous post if you haven't and are wondering how I got to Osaka.

We didn't really have that much time in Osaka, basically just two nights. 

First day was doing the main touristy stuff in Osaka. We went to Temma, battled a bit of rain, got some takoyaki, and had a sushi lunch, then headed back to the dottonbori area. We walked around, my friend was lookig for knives, then stopped at a game centre for some sound voltex, which I was keen to introduce to him. Luckily it was a hit, and we wasted many an hour playing together during our trip.

We did the running man, donki, and had an Okonomiyaki dinner. It was really good. Also had yakisoba. I really like that kind of meal, not sure why. Maybe the one I had with mum in March was better though. Could have just been an inflated expectations thing though. But my friend is really fussy about his diet (ie doesn't really eat pork) which makes things more inconvenient because all the best dishes in se Asia are pork ones. 

We went back home after dinner, I was feeling pretty pleased with my day's work as a tour guide. It wasn't over though, as we headed to round 1 to play more sound voltex. We left around 1am when the machines started telling us they needed to go to bed and so we had to too. It was fun.

We walked back home, a nice lady on the street offered us both a massage, and despite my tired legs I declined.

It was another really long day but a good one.

The next morning, we decided to head to the station early because Tokyo has more to offer than Osaka, and my friend was due to come back anyway to see his girlfriend. On the way I wanted to stop at 551 Horai, a steamed bun shop that they don't have in Tokyo. We got 4 pork buns for me and 12 siu mai to share.

We ate them on the bullet train back to Tokyo (yay Tokyo). It really does feel like a second home now, I'm always relieved when I come back. We went to Akihabara first, to try and drop off bags. In the end we headed straight to his accoms to try and get in early, there was a bit of a communication breakdown but we ended up leaving the big bags in the hallway outside his room. It's Tokyo, they were fine.
