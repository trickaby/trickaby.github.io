So been a bit of a whirlwind last week or so, since moving. Not because I've done a lot particularly. Started school again, and that's been a bit of an adjustment to be honest. I do miss a lot of my old classmates, they were good company and a nice mix of people. It felt relaxed but enjoyable. Now it feels a bit more like being at school where most of the kids aren't there to learn, which blows my mind as we're all adults. A lot of the students are early 20s and still behaving like teenagers, chatting away through class, pulling faces at the teacher behind her back (which is absurd, I know. It's only one person doing it but it makes me livid). There's also just fewer English speakers in total which just leaves fewer people to be friends with.

But it's not all bad, I don't want to come across as not enjoying it. I still really enjoy just being in a classroom learning, and the intensity has picked up. We seem to be learning quicker, and getting more homework, and learning more kanji. I'm definitely studying more and I've got more intent while I do so. I am hopeful that I'll improve a decent amount in the next 3 months.

I'm also in a different building to last term. It means the people I normally have lunch with are 10 minutes walk away, so I've got to go find them, or they send me a link to where they've decided to go to eat. Again it's fine, but a bit worse than last term.

My new living arrangements are much better though! I'm in a worse room, with less space, having to share a dirtier kitchen, and a smellier bathroom. But I'm enjoying it more, I feel more relaxed. Sometimes I struggle to get out and about and talk to people. While I still have a tendency to stay in my room too much, it's pretty easier to pop down to the kitchen and find someone to chat to, or just sit on the sofa and watch things unfold.

Also joined a nearby gym. It was about time. It's not the greatest though, a bit small. Still, better than nothing. There's not as many parks near me so not really a chance to exercise outdoors.

There are a couple of people moving out already, which is a shame as I was getting along with them quite well. It's nothing dramatic, I think this is just when they were scheduled to move out. Nevertheless it's always tough to hang out with people knowing it'll be short lived. Guess I'll have to get used to making friends only for them to eventually disappear. Happened at the end of last term and it'll happen again at the end of this, although I suspect it'll be even worse. I suppose stepping back it's a good thing, but still. Won't be fun.

I am feeling a bit of time pressure now, I guess I'm around halfway through my stay? Looking at booking a flight back home and closing this chapter eventually. Makes me feel like I have to make the most of my time here, which is annoying because I don't really know what that entails. Or even what I want from my time here. Anyway, it gives me a stomach ache.

I'm still going through a bit of a wake-up-anxious cycle, with some days better than others. Weirdly I think the world cup really isn't helping. Sometimes I'll wake a 4, 5am. sometimes there's no games and I'll lie in until 7 which is just as bad. Back home what really helped was my routine of getting up at 6 every morning, doing some exercise and getting on with my day. Now it's all a bit shuffled around. 

Also not eating that well, initially I was a bit too shy to use the kitchen to make anything complicated, so I'd just snack on nuts or buy something from the conbini. It's still not fully better, but I'm cooking my own meals and stuff. I'm also not getting sick which is encouraging. When I do eat it's pretty healthy so that's good. 

But yeah some times after class I'll be in a really bad mood, which isn't good! Today it was because of some of the students being rude and noisy. Other days I'll have no problem and be in a great mood. It's strange. I was talking to a classmate about this, and we reckon it's because we feel time is running down, so we're just more emotionally invested. It's like stoppage time when you're a goal down.

I also missed football last week to go to the sharehouse leaving/birthday party. Usually football is good for regulating my mood a bit. Lets me blow off some steam.

Got quite a busy weekend to look forward to. A housemate is hosting a futsal meetup which I'm going to on Saturday. Also world cup Saturday and Sunday morning. Then karaoke and dinner on Sunday night. I'm also pencilled in for my normal Sunday football but that's likely to be skipped in favour of karaoke, depending on start times.

So yeah, over the last few weeks I've been a bit up and down, but on average I guess roughly ok? I'm still having to remind myself I'm on a once in a lifetime holiday, and it's way better than being at work 9-5. Still, it's weird how the brain just adjusts its definition of normal so easily. 6 months ago I was dreaming of the chance to be able to just pour all my time into learning Japanese and exploring the country. I should remind myself of how cool it is.

Weirdly I don't think I've talked much about my overall impressions of Japan. It's a tough one because you get used to things, and forget what you've now taken for granted.

It's a bit silly but the conbini is a huge part of Japan living, it really changes how you go about your life. Just removes a lot of the friction if you're peckish, or feeling a bit low on energy, or thirsty. They're everywhere, and have genuinely interesting and tasty things. The fried chicken, soft drinks, random desserts and sandwiches, onigiri. Protein drinks and bars for exercising people. Bento lunches if you're too tired to cook or pressed for time. The UK alternative would probably just be a small tescos or something to get a meal deal, which in comparison just seems a bit depressing.

Trains are way better. They just show up when they're supposed to, and are adequately scheduled to handle the passenger volume. Also cleaner and air conditioned.

The people are friendlier, obviously. You don't worry about if a person looks a bit sketchy, or tap your pockets periodically to make sure your phone is still there. You can walk around at night without having to worry. Although I did get manhandled once by a guy outside a pub who was calling my Zlatan. In fairness the guy wasn't Japanese. That being said, I don't get much of an impression of the people being that much warmer. Just way more polite. I do like how when there's construction going on, there's a bunch of security personnel shepherding people around the cordoned off areas. They tend to give a little bow of the head as you pass too. 

Food is better and cheaper. But not everything! Fruits and veggies are way cheaper back home, and I think just as good. But yeah eating out is way better. You can just wander into a restaurant on the street and it's probably going to be good and decently priced. I feel like in the UK you'd have to search out stuff a bit more to avoid getting fleeced.

I don't want to go on too much about how much cheaper things are here. A big part of it is that wages are lower here, and Japanese people are struggling relatively. For them, a £15 sushi meal is expensive and indulgent, whereas for us it's a bargain. In general though, things are priced more fairly for the working person in my opinion, things like rent and travel, and food for the most part. As with most parts of the world though, it's getting worse.

I've been enjoying getting a bit more writing done of late. Despite the heavy school workload, it feels like I've got more time these days. Probably because there's less football on to be honest. I'm quietly looking forward to it being over, then I can get my mornings back! In case anyone's wondering, I missed the England game bacause I was at school. My friend keeps reminding me that I could've just skipped a day, but somehow that never feels like an option for me.

Ok I'll leave it here, hope everyone is enjoying the weather. It's getting hot here now too. Rainy season looks to be over.
