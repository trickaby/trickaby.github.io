England is having a drought at the moment, meanwhile I write this having just barely escaped a downpour on my way back from the gym. It's chucking it down pretty heavily now, gone full blown thunderstorm. It's rained a fair amount here recently, and on balance it appears that I've got the better of the weather here in Tokyo than back home. I was not expecting that, the Tokyo summer is notoriously harsh but seems like the UK, and a lot of Europe it seems, has been worse. I was supposed to do a food shop straight after gym, but that's been scuppered. Hopefully the rain calms down a bit because otherwise it'll be slim pickings for dinner.

Anyway, what have I been up to? Not a lot, went to the beach again with sharehouse people last weekend. Someone was moving out and wanted to do a day out together, it was nice. Did a bit of swimming too, there were fishes jumping out of the water while I was swimming which was really cool. I was telling my friends about it and they didn't seem super interested, but then when I pointed them out while we were swimming, they got as excited as I did. It was raining just before we arrived, so the sand was a bit damp but the air was cooler, which I was glad about. We arrived fairly late in the day (by my standards), but stayed long enough to see the sun set behind some thick cloud cover. Was still cool though.

I've still got a bit of a cough which is annoying, it's not bad at all but just frustrating it's hung around this long. I'm back able to do all my normal things though which is the main thing. 

Some of my friends from school are really into this arcade game, sound voltex. It's a bit like guitar hero, but on a big arcade console. There's 6 buttons and 2 dials, and you just have to press/turn them in time with the music. Some of my friends play pretty much every day after school and are insanely good. I went along this week for the first time, because one of my friends from class was heading home, and annoyingly enjoyed it quite a lot. As a parting gift he set me up with a card which saves your progress. You can also load money onto it so you don't have to pay with coins each time. I'm all out of hyakuens as a result of my trial run, so I guess it's good to have some digital currency.

It was really fun though, in all honesty. Partly it was nice to hang out with my friend one last time, along with a few others of us. But also the game itself was good, and it's a nice thing to be able to do together. I was rubbish at first, obviously, but you develop some coordination pretty quick. It's also a little extra exposure to Japanese media, which I've been lagging on a bit recently. Hopefully I'll discover some new music I like from playing the game.

I've also been looking for something new to keep me entertained, so maybe this is it. I guess I've been here a while so maybe got a bit too relaxed and lost some adventurous spirit. I don't know if you can call this being adventurous but at least it's getting me out of the house and away from my screens, into another indoor space in front of a different, larger screen.

Also went to gyoza no osho a few times. It's a standard Japanese Chinese food chain, they do fried rice and fried chicken and gyoza and the like. It's akin to a Chinese takeaway type restaurant back home. They even do sweet and sour pork. It's one of those places you wouldn't really take a tourist to, especially if they're short on time, but as a longer term local running out of ideas for where to go for lunch, it's a nice experience.

Also the Pokemon centre we went to during summer holiday, that was fun. We went for ten-don first (tendon is short for tempura-don, basically tempura on rice). It was good, but we queued for an hour to get in. We think it was some touristy place judging by the Chineseness of the queue we were in, but it was tasty nonetheless. I won't go back though, you can get great tempura, without waiting an hour, pretty easily in Tokyo. Actually one of my favourite meals with mum was just a random tempura place we stumbled into on our way back from Asakusa.

The Pokemon centre itself was cool. They had a big snorlax at the front, and a wall stuff from all the main-line games. I took a few photos but that was pretty much it. The rest was merch shopping stuff. I didn't buy anything. It was also very busy. I know it doesn't sound great and it probably wouldn't be if you're not a pokemon fan, but I had fun just walking around and seeing all the gubbins. After that we headed to Tokyo station which was nearby, and they had a pokemon store there too. The queue snaked all around the shop - the counter was at the far right, and people were queueing up all along the back and round to the front left. We didn't buy anything because the wait would probably have rivalled that of the ten-don.

Tokyo station itself is really cool. Doesn't really feel Japanese but not in a bad way. We found a shopping centre nearby, where you can go up to a little rooftop garden thing and get a better view, albeit a bit side-on. Still, it was cool.

Feel like I'm forgetting things, this is why I should write these more often. Ah well, if it was important I'd have remembered... 

I've updated my photos albums, not that there's much to update. Hope you enjoy and don't drown in wildfire
